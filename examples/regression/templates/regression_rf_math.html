CART Regression Trees

At each node, for a candidate split $s=(j,t)$ (feature $j$ thresholded at $t$), the dataset $S$ is partitioned into

$$S_L = \\{(x_i,y_i): x_{ij} \\le t\\},\\qquad S_R = \\{(x_i,y_i): x_{ij} > t\\}.$$

The node prediction is the mean response in the node: $$\\hat y(S)=\\frac{1}{|S|}\\sum_{(x_i,y_i)\\in S} y_i.$$

The (MSE) impurity is

$$\\mathrm{MSE}(S)=\\frac{1}{|S|}\\sum_{(x_i,y_i)\\in S}\\big(y_i-\\hat y(S)\\big)^2.$$

We choose the split minimizing the weighted post-split impurity:

$$\\mathrm{Score}(s)=\\frac{|S_L|}{|S|}\\,\\mathrm{MSE}(S_L)+\\frac{|S_R|}{|S|}\\,\\mathrm{MSE}(S_R).$$

The best split $s^*$ minimizes $\\mathrm{Score}(s)$. Leaves predict their mean; stopping criteria: max depth, min samples, or zero impurity gain.

Random Forest

Intuition (Plain Words)
  • A single tree can overfit (memorize). A forest grows many different trees.
  • Each tree sees a slightly different sample of data and a random subset of features at splits.
  • We average their predictions. Averaging cancels out individual tree noise, giving a smoother, more reliable prediction.
  • There is no one equation for the whole forest; it's learned step by step by splitting on features.

Bagging. Train $B$ trees on bootstrap samples. The forest prediction averages tree outputs:

$$\\hat f_{\\text{RF}}(x)=\\frac{1}{B}\\sum_{b=1}^B \\hat f_b(x).$$

Bagging reduces variance roughly by a factor related to $1/B$ and inter-tree correlation.

Feature subsampling. At each split, consider a random subset of features to decorrelate trees and further reduce variance.

OOB error. Use out-of-bag samples (not drawn in a tree's bootstrap) to estimate generalization error without a separate validation set.

Worked Example (1D Stump)

For $x=[0,1,2,3],\\; y=[0,0,1,1]$, evaluate thresholds between points and pick the best split.

Summary & Learnability

  • Tree training is learned greedily via impurity reduction (no closed-form solution).
  • Random Forest averages many de-correlated trees → strong predictive performance and variance reduction.
  • Feature importance can be defined via impurity decrease or permutation importance.
