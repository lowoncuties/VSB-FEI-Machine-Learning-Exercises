Problem Setup

Given $n$ observations and $d$ features, let $X \\in \\mathbb{R}^{n\\times d}$ (with an added column of ones for the intercept) and $\\,y \\in \\mathbb{R}^{n}$. Linear regression models the response as

$$\\hat y = X\\,\\beta \\quad \\text{with parameters } \\beta \\in \\mathbb{R}^{d}.$$

Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) chooses $\\beta$ to minimize the squared residuals:

$$J(\\beta) = \\frac{1}{2}\\,\\lVert y - X\\beta \\rVert_2^2 = \\frac{1}{2}(y - X\\beta)^\\top (y - X\\beta).$$

Intuition (Plain Words)

  • We have input numbers (features) and an output number for each example.
  • We draw a straight line (or a flat plane/hyperplane for many features) that best goes through all points.
  • "Best" means the total squared vertical distances between the points and the line is as small as possible.
  • The formula finds the exact line that minimizes this total error. That's why OLS has a closed-form answer.
  • If features are on very different scales or highly correlated, we stabilize the fit using Ridge regularization.

Derivation (Normal Equations)

Take the gradient and set to zero:

$$\\nabla J(\\beta) = -X^\\top(y - X\\beta) = X^\\top X\\,\\beta - X^\\top y \\stackrel{!}{=} 0.$$

Hence the normal equations:

$$X^\\top X\\,\\beta = X^\\top y \\quad \\Rightarrow \\quad \\beta = (X^\\top X)^{-1} X^\\top y\\quad (\\text{if }X^\\top X\\text{ invertible}).$$

Convexity. The Hessian is $\\nabla^2 J(\\beta)=X^\\top X\\succeq 0$, so $J$ is convex and global minimizers are unique when $X^\\top X$ is positive definite.

Geometric view. $\\hat y = X\\hat\\beta$ is the orthogonal projection of $y$ onto the column space of $X$.

Regularization & Pseudoinverse

Ridge (L2). Add $\\tfrac{\\lambda}{2}\\lVert\\beta\\rVert_2^2$:

$$\\beta_{\\text{ridge}}=(X^\\top X+\\lambda I)^{-1}X^\\top y,$$

well-conditioned even when $X^\\top X$ is singular.

Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse. The minimum-norm OLS solution is $\\beta=X^\\dagger y$ with $X^\\dagger=(X^\\top X)^{-1}X^\\top$ when full rank.

Statistics & Assumptions (OLS)

  • Linear model: $y=X\\beta+\\varepsilon$ with $\\mathbb{E}[\\varepsilon]=0$, $\\mathrm{Var}(\\varepsilon)=\\sigma^2 I$.
  • Unbiasedness: $\\mathbb{E}[\\hat\\beta]=\\beta$ under Gauss–Markov assumptions.
  • Variance: $\\mathrm{Var}(\\hat\\beta)=\\sigma^2(X^\\top X)^{-1}$.
  • Prediction: $\\hat y = X\\hat\\beta$, residuals $r=y-\\hat y$.

Worked Example (1D with Intercept)

We compute $\\beta_1 = S_{xy}/S_{xx}$ and $\\beta_0 = \\bar y - \\beta_1\\,\\bar x$ for points $\\{(x_i,y_i)\\}$.

Summary

  • OLS minimizes squared error; convex objective with closed-form solution.
  • Ridge regression stabilizes when multicollinearity is present.
  • Geometrically: projection of $y$ onto span$(X)$.
